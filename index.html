🌿 Network Outreach Opening

Warm calls to organizations connected through Rev. Woodberry's network
📌 Why this call works: You are calling on behalf of Rev. Leo Woodberry — a 30+ year community organizer, pastor of Kingdom Living Temple, ED of New Alpha CDC, recipient of President Biden's Lifetime Achievement Award, lead organizer of the 10-state Justice First Tour, and advisor to Sierra Club's Ready for 100 Campaign. His name carries weight in this network. Lead with it.
💡 Mindset: This is not a cold call. The person you're calling either knows Rev. Woodberry directly, has worked alongside New Alpha CDC, or has been part of a coalition he's organized. Lead with relationship, place yourself within his world, and remember — your job in the first 30 seconds is to make them glad you called.

Step 1 — Confirm the Right Person

SAY
"Hi, is this [Contact Name]? Wonderful — this is [Your Name] calling on behalf of Rev. Leo Woodberry and the team at Woodberry Strategic Solutions. Did I catch you at an okay moment, or is now not great?"
✦ "Calling on behalf of Rev. Woodberry" is the magic phrase. It signals warm relationship, not cold sales. Always offer the out — Rev. Woodberry's name earns courtesy, not entitlement to their time.

Step 2 — Place Yourself in Their World

CONTEXT
"The reason I'm reaching out — Rev. Woodberry has been doing community development and environmental justice work out of Florence, South Carolina for over 30 years through New Alpha CDC and Kingdom Living Temple, and [Org Name] has come up in his network through [shared coalition / Justice First / Sierra Club / NEJAC / Green Faith / mutual contact]. He asked me to personally reach out to organizations he respects to share what we've been building on the consulting side."
✦ Have one specific connection point ready. "We saw you at Justice First's gathering last fall" is a thousand times stronger than vague generality.
IF THEY ASK "REMIND ME WHO REV. WOODBERRY IS?"
"Of course — Rev. Leo Woodberry is the Executive Director of New Alpha Community Development Corporation in Florence, SC, and pastor of Kingdom Living Temple. He's been a leader in environmental and climate justice work for decades — founding member of the African American Environmental Justice Action Network back in 1994, lead organizer of the Justice First Tour across ten Southern states, advisor to the Sierra Club's Ready for 100 Campaign, and a member of the EPA's National Environmental Justice Advisory Council. He recently received President Biden's Lifetime Achievement Award for his work."
✦ Don't apologize for listing credentials — these earn the next 60 seconds. But say them as fact, not boast. Pace it like introducing a friend.

Step 3 — The Real Reason for the Call

SAY
"So here's why we're reaching out specifically. Rev. Woodberry has been providing strategic counsel — strategic planning, grant writing, capacity building, coalition organizing — to organizations across the country for years, mostly through New Alpha and informal asks. Woodberry Strategic Solutions is the consulting firm we've now formalized to do that work at scale, with a team that includes folks like Dr. Mustafa Santiago Ali, formerly of the EPA's Office of Environmental Justice, and Dr. Florence Anoruo at SC State and Brookhaven National Lab. We wanted to make sure organizations already in Rev. Woodberry's network heard about it from us first."
✦ "Heard about it first" = belonging language. The team mentions add credibility — you're not just a one-person shop.

Step 4 — Hand the Mic Back to Them

PIVOT QUESTION
"Before I tell you anything else — I'd actually rather hear from you first. What's been on your plate at [Org Name] lately? What's going well, and where are you feeling the most pressure?"
✦ STOP TALKING after the question. Whatever they say first is what they care most about — that's where you'll anchor everything that follows.
➡ Next Step: Whatever they share, that's your bridge into Discovery. Don't pitch services yet — ask one or two follow-up questions to go deeper on what they just told you.

🧭 Discovery — Listen for the Real Need

Curiosity over pitching. Their answers shape your recommendation.
💡 Mindset: The biggest mistake on a network outreach call is jumping into services too quickly. You earn the right to recommend by demonstrating you understand their world first. Aim for 70% listening, 30% talking in this section.

Frame the Conversation

SAY
"I want to be respectful of your time, so let me ask just a handful of questions that'll help me figure out if there's actually a good fit here — or if I'm better off pointing you somewhere else. Either outcome is fine with me."
✦ "Or pointing you somewhere else" = removes pressure. Builds enormous credibility. Most consultants won't say this.

Discovery Questions — Ask 3–5, Not All

MISSION & PROGRAMS
"Tell me a little about where [Org Name] is right now. What's the work look like this year compared to last year — growing, holding steady, evolving?"
FUNDING HEALTH
"On the funding side — are you mostly grant-funded, individual donor-driven, government contracts, earned revenue, or a mix? And how is that mix sitting with you right now? With everything happening around federal funding, a lot of organizations are reassessing."
✦ Reference the federal funding climate when relevant — many orgs in this network are nervous about USDA/EPA/DOE shifts.
COMMUNITY & IMPACT
"How would you describe the community you serve right now? Are you reaching the folks you want to be reaching, or is there a gap between the mission on paper and the impact on the ground?"
BOARD & GOVERNANCE
"How would you describe your board right now? Are they engaged where you need them, or is that one of those 'always working on it' areas?"
STAFF CAPACITY
"And on the staffing side — does it feel like you have the team and capacity to do what you're being asked to do? Or are folks stretched?"
COALITION & PARTNERSHIPS
"Who else are you working with on this? Are you part of any coalitions or collaboratives that are giving you leverage — or do you feel like you're carrying it alone?"
✦ This is a Rev. Woodberry signature question. Coalition-building is one of his strongest practice areas.
STRATEGIC HORIZON
"When you look out 12 to 24 months — what's the thing that, if it goes well, makes everything else easier? And what's the thing that, if it doesn't get solved, will keep you up at night?"
⭐ THE BRIDGE QUESTION
"If we could wave a magic wand and have one thing solved or strengthened at [Org Name] by this time next year — what would it be?"
✦ Their answer is your entire pitch. Whatever they say, that's the service you lead with.

Reflect Back Before Moving On

REFLECT
"So what I'm hearing — and stop me if I'm off — is that [summarize their top 1-2 challenges in their own words]. Did I get that right?"
✦ Mirroring their words back proves you listened. This single move builds more trust than any credentials slide.

🛑 Objection Navigation

Most "no"s are really "not yet" or "not like this"
💡 Mindset: Nonprofit objections are almost always rooted in budget anxiety, past bad experiences with consultants, or internal politics. Don't argue. Acknowledge → Reframe → Ask a question that moves forward.
💸 "We don't have any budget for consultants right now."
"I completely understand — and honestly, that's the most common thing we hear, especially this year. Two thoughts: first, a lot of our engagements are funded by foundations through capacity-building grants, so the org doesn't pay out of operating budget. Rev. Woodberry's relationships with funders like Ford Foundation, Kresge, and others go back decades, and we can often help orgs identify and apply for those dollars. Second, even if a full engagement isn't a fit, the one-hour introductory consultation is a much smaller commitment. Would it be worth a 30-minute call just to map out which option, if any, might fit?"
➡ Move to Close: "Would Tuesday or Thursday work better for that 30-minute call?"
🤝 "We're already working with another consultant."
"That's great to hear — really. The orgs that bring in outside expertise tend to outperform the ones that don't. Can I ask what they're helping you with? [Listen.] Got it. We tend to focus on [adjacent area — coalition building, EJ-focused strategy, federal funding, etc.], so it might not be a duplicate at all. And frankly, if it is, I'd rather know now and stay out of your way. Would it be useful to compare notes — even just have Rev. Woodberry weigh in on your strategy as a second opinion?"
➡ Move to Close: Position Rev. Woodberry's experience as the second-opinion value-add.
🏛️ "I'd have to take this to my board."
"Absolutely — and you should. That's exactly the right process. Let me make that easier for you. What if I sent you a one-page summary plus Rev. Woodberry's bio you could share with the board chair, plus a short scoping call before any cost is on the table? That way when you bring it up, you've got concrete information instead of trying to translate a phone call. Who's the right board member or committee for me to make sure the materials speak to?"
➡ Move to Close: "Should I send the one-pager today, or time it with your next board meeting?"
⏳ "We're stretched too thin to take on consulting right now."
"I hear that constantly — and I want to push back gently. The orgs that engage outside support during the stretched seasons usually come out of the stretch faster than the ones that white-knuckle through it. Some of our engagements are designed to take work off your plate — grant writing, program evaluation, and feasibility studies are good examples. Could I describe one or two of those quickly, just so you know what's available when the time is right?"
➡ Move to Services: Lead with grant writing or program evaluation — capacity-extending services.
😬 "We tried a consultant before and didn't get value."
"I really appreciate you being honest about that — and you're not alone. A lot of consulting in the nonprofit space gets sold as expertise but delivered as a generic template. I'd be curious what didn't work, if you're willing to share. [Listen carefully.] That tracks. Here's what I'd say is different about us: Rev. Woodberry built and ran New Alpha CDC for over 30 years before he was a consultant. He's not someone explaining your work to you from the outside — he's done your job. That changes the entire engagement. Would you be open to one conversation just to see if it feels different?"
➡ Move to Close: The "practitioner, not theorist" frame is your strongest comeback to past bad consulting experiences.
📧 "Can you just send me some information?"
"Absolutely — happy to. To make sure I send the right information rather than a generic packet, can I ask just two quick things? [Ask their #1 priority and decision timeline.] Perfect. I'll put together a tailored summary, plus Rev. Woodberry's bio and a couple of relevant case studies, and email it within 24 hours. And as a follow-up — would it be okay if I called or emailed in about a week to see if anything raised questions? Otherwise these things tend to disappear into the inbox void."
➡ Capture the follow-up commitment before ending the call.
🤷 "We're a small org — we're probably not who you usually work with."
"I hear that a lot, and let me debunk it. Rev. Woodberry started New Alpha CDC as a small grassroots operation in Florence, SC — he's spent his career working with small organizations, not just for the big national ones. We work across the size spectrum, and frankly, some of our most rewarding engagements have been with frontline community organizations punching way above their weight. Want me to walk you through what a scaled engagement might look like for an org your size?"
🌐 "Is this connected to New Alpha? Are you a nonprofit or a business?"
"Great question — and an important distinction. New Alpha CDC is the 501(c)(3) nonprofit Rev. Woodberry founded and runs in Florence, SC. Woodberry Strategic Solutions is a separate consulting firm where Rev. Woodberry, Dr. Mustafa Santiago Ali, Dr. Florence Anoruo, and Minister Malik Saafir provide professional consulting services. Two organizations, distinct missions — but New Alpha is essentially the proving ground for the strategies we bring to clients. We're not asking you to donate; we're a professional services firm. Does that clarify it?"
➡ Important: Some folks may be confused about whether this is a fundraising call. Always clarify when asked.
🚪 "Honestly, I'm just not interested."
"Totally fair, and I'll respect that — last thing we want is to be the call you dread. Can I ask one quick thing before I let you go? Is it that the timing is off, or is it more that what we do isn't relevant to where you are? I ask because if it's the second one, I'd rather take you off our outreach list and not bother you again."
➡ Either way, honor it. If they say "timing," ask when's better. If "not relevant," thank them and remove from list.
⏰ "Call me back in a few months."
"Happy to. So I'm not just guessing — what changes in a few months that makes this a better conversation? Is it the budget cycle, the board calendar, an end of fiscal year? That'll help me reach out at the right moment instead of randomly. Should we put a specific week on the calendar — say, the first week of [month]?"
➡ Schedule a real callback. Vague "in a few months" = lost lead. Specific date = warm pipeline.

Universal Bridge — Always End an Objection With a Question

⭐ THE GOLDEN RULE
Every objection response ends with a question that moves forward. Never end on a statement. Never accept a "we'll see" — convert it into a specific next step (a date, a follow-up commitment, a referral name). Whoever asks the next question controls the call.

🔐 Close — Book the Introductory Consultation

The ask is small: a one-hour intro consultation. That's it.
💡 Mindset: On a network outreach call, you are not closing a six-month engagement. You are closing the introductory consultation. The bar for "yes" is just a one-hour scheduled call with the EDs / decision team. The intro consult is where the real selling happens — let it.

The Soft Close — Book the Intro Consultation

ASK
"Based on what you just shared, I really do think there's something here worth exploring. Our introductory consultation is the easiest next step — it's one hour, no obligation, and you walk away with two or three concrete strategies you can act on regardless of whether we ever work together beyond that. Would you be open to scheduling that with Rev. Woodberry or one of our senior consultants?"
✦ "Walk away with strategies regardless" reduces resistance dramatically. The intro consultation is value-first.

The Two-Choice Close

YES/YES
"I have openings Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2. Which one works better for you? You can also book directly online at WoodberryStrategicSolutions.com if a different time works better."
✦ Never ask "would you like to schedule something?" — that's a yes/no. Always offer two specific options.
FOLLOW-UP
"Perfect. Should I include anyone else on your end — your board chair, development director, anyone you'd want in the room?"
✦ Multi-stakeholder calls = much higher conversion. Always invite the decision-makers.

Light Close — When They're Hesitant on a Meeting

FALLBACK
"No problem at all. Let me do this — I'll send you a one-page summary based on our conversation, plus Rev. Woodberry's bio and a couple of case studies of orgs in similar situations. Can I follow up in about 10 days to see if anything sparked questions?"
✦ A one-pager + scheduled follow-up keeps the relationship alive without forcing a meeting.
